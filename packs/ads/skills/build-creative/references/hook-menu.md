# The hook menu

A hook is the promise in the first seconds that earns the next seconds. Two axes combine: the hook type (the move) and the market's awareness stage (how much the viewer already knows). Pick the stage, pick a type, write the line. To produce eight hooks fast, take eight types and write one line each.

Every example here is written for this page, across varied products. Study the move, then write your own for your offer. Examples are SEND blocks: short, native, sound-off legible, one idea, no fabricated proof.

## The awareness ladder: pick the stage first

Be one stage above the market unless an identity niche lets you jump in. Attach to a desire already present; never try to manufacture one. Write to one stage per ad.

| stage | the viewer | the move |
|---|---|---|
| most aware | knows you and the offer | straight offer or news: "new sizes in", "the spring cohort opens Monday" (real dates only) |
| product aware | knows your product, weighing it | answer the top question, meet the objection, a balanced testimonial, a factual comparison; one video per top customer question |
| solution aware | knows solutions exist, not yours | "a faster way to [outcome]", "[product] for less than [the expensive alternative]", "[outcome] without [the thing they hate]" |
| problem aware | feels the problem, hunting a fix | name the pain, promise relief without the thing they hate |
| unaware | does not yet name the problem | symptom, then problem, then solution, then product, then offer; a story, not a one-liner |

In a sophisticated market (stages four and five), the "without [the things they already tried]" construction carries the subhead. Order the alternatives by how often buyers really tried them, not by anecdote.

## The hook types

1. Demographic. Name the buyer so broad delivery finds them: age, job title, lifestyle, condition. The most under-used type on broad targeting.
   `Night-shift nurses. Your meal prep is doing this to your sleep.`
   `Owners of one-van plumbing firms. Here's where your Fridays go.`

2. Problem-led. Lead with the pain, promise relief without the thing they hate.
   `Still re-keying invoices into the bank every Sunday?`
   `Cracked heels in winter, without the overnight socks.`

3. Proof, authority or statistic. Lead with evidence. A balanced testimonial, authority by experience, a hard number, a study. The proof must be real; a placeholder stands in until the owner supplies it.
   `{{count}} bike shops switched their booking to us last year. Here's what changed.`
   `I serviced boilers for eleven winters. This is the valve that fails first.`

4. Curiosity or mechanism. Open a loop, name a hidden mechanism.
   `Your sourdough isn't failing because of the flour.`
   `The reason your payroll runs late is a date field nobody looks at.`

5. Conversational or native. Read like a conversation already in progress, like content not an ad.
   `No because why did nobody tell me a tyre pump could do this.`
   `Okay the way this spreadsheet just fixed my whole week.`

6. Anti-traditional. Call out the normal way and challenge it, often with a before and after. Use when the product requires changing a habit.
   `Stop hiring a bookkeeper for a business that makes twelve invoices a month.`
   `Stop stretching before you run. Do this instead.`

7. Controversy. A common enemy, a hot take, a debate. Use when challenging a norm. An honest contrast, never a manufactured enemy or a claim against a competitor you cannot prove.
   `Gym memberships are built so you don't show up.`
   `Most "organic" dog food is the same kibble in a kinder bag. Ask for the batch sheet.`

8. Objection. Meet the doubt head-on. Use when the viewer already has uncertainty.
   `How do I know if my receipts are even tidy enough to hand over?`
   `How do I know if this language app will stick after week two?`

9. Speed and transformation. A time phrase plus a before and after. No claims the product cannot deliver; no clinical outcomes.
   `A patched inner tube in four minutes, no levers.`
   `A clean set of books by Friday, from a shoebox of receipts.`

10. Audience callout, point of view, native trend. Insert a relatable statement into a native format.
    `POV: you went from dreading month-end to closing it before lunch.`
    `POV: your toddler's lunchbox came home empty for the first time.`

11. Trigger moment. One person, just after one costly event, with the figure. The reader in that moment sees themselves in the first line. Put documented facts in before publishing.
    `You just filed your first return as a contractor and you owe {{real figure}}.`
    `The venue cancelled {{days}} before your wedding. The deposit was {{real figure}}.`

Stacking: a multi-hook opener strings two or three types (demographic plus controversy, demographic plus anti-traditional). Useful but secondary; get one type landing before stacking.

## A worked set: eight types, one offer

Offer: a scheduling tool for small dental practices. Problem-aware market. SEND blocks; the proof line carries a placeholder for a real result.

> Demographic: `Practice managers with one receptionist. Your diary is the bottleneck, not the dentist.`
> Problem-led: `Three no-shows a day and nobody to fill the gaps. The diary is the problem.`
> Proof: `{{count}} practices cut no-shows last quarter with one reminder change. Here's the change.`
> Curiosity: `Your no-shows aren't random. They cluster on one day, and your diary can't see it.`
> Conversational: `Okay so why is every dental diary still a paper book with a screen on top.`
> Anti-traditional: `Stop calling patients the day before. It's too late by then.`
> Objection: `How do I know if my patients will even read a text reminder?`
> Comparison, factual: `A scheduling tool costs less than one missed crown appointment a week.`

Eight types, one offer, no invented numbers.

## Prompt patterns for widening the pool

Use a model to widen, rank and vary. A human, or the campaign's reviewer, judges every output against the honesty floor and the voice guide.

- Generate from a customer bio. Give the model the buyer's bio (their day, their words, their workaround) and this ladder. Ask for one hook per type at one named stage.
- Rank for likely conversion. Give the hook set and the persona. Ask the model, as a performance marketer, to order them from most to least likely to convert and to say why for each.
- Vary from a winner. Paste the winning ad's transcript. Ask for ten genuinely different scripts around the same desire and persona, each changing one lever: desire, awareness, sophistication, or persona.
- Sharpen the top hooks. Give the best three. Ask for versions that land harder on the feeling, with warmth, without shame, and without any claim the product cannot deliver.
- Read the split. If an ad spends but does not convert, the hook works and the back half needs work. If it gets little spend, rework the hook.

## The reposted story

Quote or paraphrase a stranger's public post, link the thread, add your own read (what it cost, what fixes it). That read is what makes it your post. Never tell it as your own experience or as a customer's; that is fabricated proof.
