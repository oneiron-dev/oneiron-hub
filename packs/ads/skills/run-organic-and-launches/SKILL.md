# Run organic and launches

You run the channels that cost time instead of money: a standing short-form engine, text posts, carousels, launch spikes and free tools. Whether a channel fits the offer at all is `plan-the-channel`. Hooks and the moment bank are `build-creative`. The pack floor in `PACK.md` applies throughout. Most of this field is one-practitioner experience; every platform mechanic is perishable and every view floor is yours to set.

## Use this skill when

- A consumer or show-able product wants a free distribution engine.
- A founder wants to post on a text platform and does not know if the buyer is there.
- A launch is coming on a maker, forum or product-listing surface.
- A free single-feature tool is on the table.
- An account is new, or a trend is tempting.

## First lesson: fit has three parts

A format works only when it clears a view floor you set, earns comments, and drives product traffic. Views with no comments and no clicks are vanity. There is no point finding a format that gets millions of views and brings no revenue.

Do not judge a format search before a large sample. Set the window up front: on the order of a hundred posts across formats is a planning assumption you choose, not a validated minimum. A scheduler can post; it cannot read the results and change the format. That weekly human read is the work.

## Procedure

### 1. Organic short-form as a recurring channel

Views reach customers through a leaky chain: post, profile, bio, landing page, signup or purchase, active or paying user. A million-view video into a dead bio converts nobody. Two moves inside the chain:

- Demonstrate, don't describe. Screen recordings, before and after, real usage beat narrated features.
- The bio is a conversion mechanism, not a self-description. It answers what the product does, why the viewer should go, and a path to get there. Where a new account cannot link, type the URL in plain text. Link-stripping behaviour per platform is perishable; verify.

Named formats are a menu, not a method. Treat each (a replay-loop clip, a skit, a reaction) as one format among several to test. A format that uses film footage or a real actor's likeness carries copyright and likeness risk; flag it before building on it.

### 2. Text posts on a feed platform

Decide fit first. Pull about fifty recent posts in the problem's own words and count how many come from someone who has the problem, not vendors, commentators or news. Where the buyer is not talking there, the platform is for credibility and peers, not sales. A revenue screenshot posted on the platform is not a sale made by the platform.

A named person beats a page. The located wins for products and services have a visible person posting and replying. A faceless niche page has no corroborated sales case; run one only as a pre-registered cheap test.

Post trigger moments, not motivation: one person, one costly event, one figure, the feeling now. Directional evidence favours the format over generic motivation; none shows it draws more buyers on its own. Judge on paying buyers.

Schedule natively or through the official API. Write replies by hand. Posting outside the app or the official API risks suspension of every linked account (dated row). Official access is not a licence to spam.

Run it as a format search: a window chosen up front, a kill line, a weekly read of the chain (reach, profile visits, link clicks, paying buyers minus refunds), a tagged link per format.

Links: write the post so it stands alone, put the link where it reads best, and test placement on clicks. The "links in replies" advice is dated (`knowledge/stale-registry.md`).

Reposting a forum story is fine with the thread linked and your own read added. Never tell it as your own or a customer's.

### 3. Carousels

An image carousel is organic short-form with the same three-part bar. The method: mine winners first; adapt the structure, write your own words. Details in `references/launches-and-carousels.md`.

### 4. Launch spikes

A launch on a community surface is a burst of high-intent traffic if the listing is prepared as a sales page. It converts a small waitlist into first customers; it is not a pipeline. The listing, the per-community reframe, the founder's first comment, the tagline memory test and the cadence are in `references/launches-and-carousels.md`. Never ask for upvotes; coordinated voting gets detected and punished.

Every feature is a launch, but launch only real news. A launch with nothing new spends the audience's trust. Each launch prints creative and touch points that paid reuses.

### 5. Operator plays, self-reported

Carry these with their flags: each is one operator's account, not measured against a control.

- A story system for forums: warm the account by participating for weeks; story-plus-proof posts in niche groups, never a pitch in the post; recognition builds over several posts; reply to every comment; handle hostility in public. It works inside dwell-don't-sell only because the posts are genuinely valuable. No fake accounts, no vote rings.
- Build in public for services: process-over-polish posts a few times a week, one primary surface plus cross-posting, metrics ignored in the cold-start months. It converts for services (the buyer hires the visible process) faster than for products.
- A two-surface limit for a solo creator: short-form tests ideas; winners graduate to long-form and new channels only from validated material.
- An owned email list turns a one-off launch into a repeatable event. Every launch surface captures emails, not only installs.
- A comparison page per competitor, and watch which pages AI answers cite. The craft belongs to the search pack.
- Test distribution before building: post about the pain from a small account; no breakout after many tries kills the channel for that app.

### 6. Free-tool marketing

Ship a tiny, single-feature free tool that attracts a wider audience and routes the right users to the paid product. One feature, extremely simple; list five candidates and pick the clearest name and fastest build. Free, because that is what creates the loop; a paywalled "free" tool kills it. The name says exactly what it does. Value starts on the homepage: no signup, no maze; collect emails only after value.

It pays off three ways. It widens the search surface to terms the paid product would never rank for. It is shareable. It funnels to the paid product as the natural next step. The best tool is the one the customer needs right before they need your product. Name whether a given tool is a validation play (does anyone want this feature?) or an acquisition play (bring strangers repeatedly). Same artifact, two purposes.

### 7. New accounts

A new account has a cold-start problem: the platform classifies you by what you engage with before it trusts what you post. Engage in the niche first, behave like a person, post from the app at first. Specific warm-up windows, daily minutes and account-per-device rules are one practitioner's checklist; offer them as something to verify against your own results, never as fact. No alias tricks to pass uniqueness checks, no multi-account tooling.

### 8. Trends

Trends are a timing supplement, not a strategy. Cap trend content at one or two posts a week. Blend the trend into a proven format rather than posting pure trend content, so new viewers land in the catalogue instead of on a one-off. One hit lifts the whole back catalogue on a newer account, which is why consistency matters. Catch it early, adapt fast; the specific windows are perishable.

## Anti-patterns

- Judging on views alone.
- Killing a format search after a dozen posts.
- Set-and-forget scheduling with no weekly read.
- Unofficial automation or automated replies on the feed platform.
- Choosing a platform because the founder is there, not the buyer.
- Optimising one link of the chain and leaving a dead bio.
- Quoting one practitioner's platform numbers as fact.
- A launch listing as a link dump; the same pitch pasted across communities.
- Skipping the founder's first comment; soliciting upvotes.
- Paywalling the free tool.
- Building on a format with uncleared footage or likeness rights.
- Letting trends replace proven formats.
- Inventing carousel hooks from your own priors instead of mining real winners.

## Checks before you return

- The format verdict cites the view floor, comments and product traffic, over the pre-set window.
- The buyer-density sample for a text platform is stated with its count.
- Every platform mechanic carries a date and a verify flag.
- The launch has a sales-page listing, a per-community reframe and a drafted first comment.
- Nothing asks for votes, uses a fake account, or evades a platform's checks.
