# Launches and carousels

## Launch spikes on community surfaces

A launch on a product-listing site, a maker forum or a discussion community is a spike event. Prepared well, it produces a burst of high-intent traffic. It converts a small waitlist into first customers; it is not a pipeline.

### A listing is a sales page

Whatever the surface, framing and presentation decide whether browsers convert. On a product-listing site: gallery images that show one feature per image, zoomed like a thumbnail, and existing social proof (a few users or testimonials) visible before you send the spike. Badge mechanics, animated logos and the best launch day are perishable platform mechanics; verify before leaning on them.

### Reframe per community

One pitch does not transfer. The two that differ most from a generic launch:

- The maker forum wants a builder's framing. Prefix the title as a maker post (a dated row). Write in first person: "I made", "after six months". Put the concrete payoff in the title, because the click decision happens there. Even a non-technical product needs an explicit technical angle or the developer audience ignores it. Titles short, no emoji.
- The discussion community punishes obvious promotion. The post must be useful or interesting on its own. The link goes in the first comment with context, not in the title, and only where the community's rules allow. Join and comment in the niche community before you post. Dwell, don't sell.

### The founder's first comment

Write your own first comment before the launch goes live, covering four things: who you are, why you built it, what trade-offs you made, and what specific feedback you want. It sets the conversational frame before anyone else replies. The title earns the click; the first comment earns the conversation. Two copy jobs; draft both.

Then reply live and honestly for the first hours. Early genuine engagement is also a signal the platform reads. Never solicit upvotes or send a direct submission link asking for support.

### The tagline memory test

For a tagline or launch title, write ten options and keep the one you can still recall after 24 hours. Unaided next-day recall is a cheap proxy for stickiness. Keep the reasoning apart from the copy:

> PREP: picking a maker-forum title for a receipt-scanning tool. Ten written, the one recalled cold the next morning kept; payoff before the click, no emoji, short.
>
> SEND: `Show HN: Turn a shoebox of receipts into a clean ledger`

### Cadence

A product that ships often treats every feature, use case and new product as its own launch: a short video plus amplification, as often as there is real news. At founder scale a launch is a founder-cut video and a thread. Launch only real news.

## Carousels

An image carousel is organic short-form: the same bar (view floor, saves or comments, product traffic) and the same large-sample patience. The craft below is durable; platform mechanics are perishable.

### Mine, then execute better

A carousel is an assembly-and-adaptation job over real winning posts, not a writing job. Models are poor at culture, hooks and trends from their own priors; they are good at reading what is already breaking out, finding the pattern, and executing it better. Spend creativity on strategy (direction, when to double down, how to read the stats). Adapt the craft (hook shape, slide structure, caption pattern, format) from proven posts, and write your own words.

### What a win decomposes into

Views come from three things, each verified against real data: a scroll-stopping first slide (hook plus visual), an angle that is trending now, and a payoff that earns the save, share or comment. A draft that cannot point to all three is not done.

### Structure by mimicry, words your own

No fixed slide count. Pull several real winning carousels in your lane, read every slide, and mirror the slide-by-slide skeleton of a proven one: a hook slide, body slides that hold the swipe, a payoff slide. Adapt the structure; write your own copy. Lifting another account's words invites a copyright complaint and reads as theirs.

### The hook board

Do not write slide one from your head; it comes out generic. Collect a ranked board of real first-slide hooks, strongest first, each paired with the mechanism behind it and the source link. Pick the strongest shape and write your own line in it. Slide one passes one test at preview: does it stop the scroll?

### Copy editing pass

Before publishing, send your draft hooks, slide copy and caption to a model as "my draft to fix" and ask it to rate and rewrite for native voice. Every run, every post. The reviewer then checks it against the voice guide.

### Images

Image-to-image off a real reference slide for a native look. Text-to-image only for faceless, object or food shots. Any image with a visible person comes from image-to-image off a real reference; generated people read fake. Generate slide one, then reference that image on every later slide so the person, scene and style hold. Prompt for a candid phone photo, real setting, natural light, grain. Be critical of waxy skin, mangled hands and uncanny faces. Text is always an overlay, never baked into the image; image models garble real text. A white pill behind the text is the safe default.

### Preview gate

Render the finished slides and look. Slide one stops the scroll; text is readable; the flow makes you swipe. Fix weak slides and re-preview before publishing.

### Platform mechanics, perishable

A trending in-lane sound is the most load-bearing reach lever on at least one platform; attach one from a winning post. Full caption with tags. Cadence is yours to set from account health. A vision-capable model is needed so the agent can see references and its own output.

### Operate like an operator

Distribution is noisy; a good post can flop by chance, and the platform often needs several reps of a format before it catches. One flop is not a dead format. Watch engagement rate relative to views, not raw views. Leave a short note after each run (what you posted, the reasoning, what you observed) and load the last several back in next time. The edge compounds across runs.

The method is language-agnostic: point the same loop at another culture's corpus, mine that market's breakout carousels, adapt their structures, attach that market's trending sound. The hard part is a research source with real slide text and sound coverage for that market.
