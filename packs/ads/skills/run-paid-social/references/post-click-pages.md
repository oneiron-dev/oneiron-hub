# Post-click pages: the page each ad sends people to

The creative does the targeting; the page finishes the job. Map a competitor's pages from the public ad library the same way you mine their ads, then build your own in an order that fits the budget.

## Map a funnel from the ad library

Open every landing URL in a competitor's library and count how many ads point at each one. The count shows where the advertiser concentrates its creative effort; it is not a spend figure. Record each ad's start date. The oldest live ads point at the page the advertiser keeps paying for: sustained use, which makes it the first page to study, not a proven winner. The library shows no spend, no return and no budget.

A typical map for a self-serve product: most ads point at the homepage; a slice at segment pages; a few at creator paths; one cluster at a competitor-switch page; a brand-story page with no button; one or two stripped sales pages with no menu.

## Patterns worth copying

- The homepage can be the lander when the product explains itself in one line: one headline, one button.
- The button names the visitor's device. A phone sees the phone store; a laptop sees the desktop download. Nobody sees a button for the wrong platform.
- One template, a new headline per buyer. Keep the layout. Swap the headline, the in-product examples and the quotes. A page for accountants shows a ledger; a page for nurses shows a shift roster; a page for a market adds a language line and a live sign-up counter for that market. This is the post-click half of "segment by creative, not by ad set".
- One path per creator. The creator's path is the homepage plus one banner carrying their code and offer. The biggest creator gets a full page with their name and quote above the button. Creators run the ads from their own handle as a paid partnership, pointing at their path. One homepage serves every creator; only the banner changes. The deal side belongs to the crm pack's creator-program preset.
- A switch page aimed at one competitor: credit for the rest of the visitor's paid time with the competitor, a bonus period, a comparison table, an email-only ask with no card. It removes the money reason to stay. Every comparison row must be provable; `write-ad-copy` bans claims you cannot back.
- A stripped page for one ad set when the main page sells too broadly: no menu, one button, one before-and-after, one pain number, one or two proof numbers, all real.
- A brand bet, judged as one. A story page with no download button at the top is judged on reach and branded search, not installs. Keep it a small share of spend.

## Small-budget order

1. Start with the homepage: one headline, a device-aware button.
2. Add one stripped page for the best ad set.
3. Add segment headlines once a segment's creative wins.
4. Add creator paths when the first creator deal signs.
5. Build a switch page only when one competitor clearly owns the buyers you want.

## Two rules that travel

- Never split-test the first page your ads point to. Editing the page the platform crawls most corrupts both the test and the control. Test with entirely new post ids pointing at a new URL, same creatives. Downstream pages (the sales page after an advertorial) split-test fine.
- Traffic temperature sets ad directness. Indirect ads (native statics, long copy) buy cheaper clicks and higher click-through, but they need an indirect funnel (advertorial, listicle, quiz) before the sales page. Direct, offer-heavy creative goes straight to the product page. A mismatch reads as a landing-page problem when it is funnel architecture.
