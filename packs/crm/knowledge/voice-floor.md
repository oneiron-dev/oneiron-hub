# The voice floor

Every outbound message keeps this floor. The campaign's voice guide sits on top of it. The reviewer reads both before a send.

## PREP and SEND

- Everything a reader sees goes in a SEND block. It must read complete and natural as is.
- Everything else is PREP: research notes, fallbacks, sequencing, coaching. PREP never ships.
- Placeholders in SEND are minimal, formatted `{{like_this}}`, and only for facts the owner supplies.
- Count the words of every SEND block. Over the cap, cut self-description first, then context, then pleasantries. One ask survives.

## Word caps (defaults; the voice guide may tighten them)

| message | cap |
|---|---|
| cold first touch | 90 |
| bump on the same thread | 70 |
| re-engagement after silence | 50 |
| research-interview ask | 120 |
| objection or discount reply | 120 |
| price-increase notice | 150 |

## Write like a small, direct human

- Plain sentences. Contractions are fine. One thought per line.
- No headers, no bold, no bullet essays inside an email.
- One ask per message. Say it once.
- The first line is about them. Never about you or your company.
- Ask for interest, not for time. The link comes after they reply.
- Uneven sentence length is fine. Perfect symmetry reads as a template.
- One real observation stated as your own read. One sentence that risks being wrong.
- Em dashes are off by default. A voice guide may turn them on.

## Tells the reviewer rejects

Any of these fails the review:

- Promo words: pivotal, game-changer, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, unlock.
- The rule of three: "faster, cheaper, and smarter."
- "Not just X, but Y."
- Stacked hedges: "could potentially help."
- "-ing" tails: "...ensuring your team stays ahead."
- Vague attributions: "industry reports suggest."
- False ranges: "from startups to enterprises."
- Fancy ways to say "is": "serves as," "boasts."
- Metaphor nouns: north star, flywheel, wedge, lever.
- Feel-claims: "streamlines operations." Say what it does or the number.
- Chatbot closers: "Hope this helps," "Let me know if you have any questions."
- A greeting about their wellbeing. A booking link in touch one. Any link in touch one.
- Profile text repeated back as research. A wrong fact about them.
- The word "AI" in a cold email, unless the product is the point.
- A cadence identical to the last fifty mails they got.

## The thousand-people screen

If the message could go to a thousand people, it fails. Personalisation that does not name something only this person has is decoration.

## Proof

- Social proof is the behaviour of similar customers, matched on role, then size, then industry, then pain.
- Never invent a case, a client, a metric or a track record. Use true anonymised proof or lead with a diagnosis.
- A testimonial opens on the exact doubt it answers.
- A guarantee may travel in cold outreach. A price does not.

## Urgency

Real only. Capacity caps, cohort dates, a real price rise, an expiring bonus. If it is real, name it exactly. If you remove an incentive on delay, remove it.

## Health and outcomes

- No outcome or clinical claims near health. Route, do not diagnose.
- Do not guarantee outcomes you do not control.
