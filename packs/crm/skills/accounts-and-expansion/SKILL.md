# Accounts and expansion

Customer success starts at close. The account's record holds the promises, the success definition, the checkpoints and the proof. Renewal is earned on proof, never bought with a discount. The live pitch and the live negotiation are `discovery-to-close`; this skill builds the proof and the readiness that make them easy.

## Procedure: onboarding

1. At close, every promise becomes a task with an owner and a date. Schedule kickoff, training, the reviews and the renewal reminder now.
2. Define success and its measure up front, in writing. Without it you cannot prove value at renewal.
3. A fast first win. Break delivery into small increments. Never leave the client unsure of the next touch.
4. The spine: a welcome note, a questionnaire, a kickoff call. Checkpoints at 30, 60 and 90 days, then 12 months.
5. A fixed-scope first job before any retainer. Land, prove, then expand.
6. Plant the expansion seed early so recurring work is not a surprise at delivery. Track return throughout.
7. Reconfirm the purchase in the first day or two. Name reasons the buyer did not. Keep small promises.

## Procedure: account health

Read health from leading signals, not from the lagging "they churned."

| signal | reading |
|---|---|
| key work not done on time | at risk |
| days since last contact rising | at risk |
| sentiment on the last touch neutral or negative | at risk |
| usage or engagement dropping | at risk |
| signed deliverables unused | prime at-risk flag |
| sponsor silent | acute |
| value never shown to them | nothing to point to at renewal |

Two or more at once: escalate a value-re-establishing reach-out now. A paying client's silence is a warning, never a cold lead. Escalate; never taper.

A tiny status sheet is enough: account, last contact, sentiment, key work done, current versus target scope. The vault assembles it from claims. Do not build a tooling project.

If churn tracks to how the deal was sold, fix selling, not onboarding.

## Procedure: the review

1. Their goals first: "when you bought, it was for X. Still the main thing?"
2. Before and after in their numbers. Never fabricate the metric. If you have none, say "let's instrument this so next quarter we can show it."
3. Issues, honestly.
4. Growth only if the account is healthy. On an unhealthy account the recap is the whole meeting.
5. Make value visible before every renewal decision, not at it.

Proactive, problem-anchored touches around the predictable issues of each implementation week beat arbitrary check-ins.

## Procedure: renewal

- A final review about three months out to surface blockers. The renewal talk about six weeks out.
- Annual terms need a renewal workflow before expiry: reminders, a survey, one-click renew.
- The last weeks weigh most. Buyers judge a recurring charge by the most recent period.
- Outcome retainers: success defined at onboarding, clear scope, a coverage boundary. Renew on proof.
- A retainer with no boundary becomes unlimited labour. Start and end dates, a renewal rule, a notice period.
- Never a reflex discount at renewal. It teaches the client to threaten churn for the next one.
- Accounts below a viable price: raise, re-scope or replace.
- If churn matches new closes, fix delivery before selling more.

## Procedure: expansion

- Earn it from delivered value. Expand into the pain the work exposed. Restraint on bolt-ons outside the trusted core.
- Signals: unused capacity, surfaced pain, a new stakeholder or tool on screen, usage growth, a repeated unprompted request.
- Act at the value moment with one specific improvement. "I have an idea to double {{their bottleneck}}. Want to hear it?" Never a menu. Never "anything else I can help with?"
- Refuse the add-on they do not need. The refusal buys the referral.
- Pick an expansion direction by current skill and reuse of the delivery process. Deepen the core before fanning out.
- Charge recurring only for recurring value without linear labour. Productise delivery. Prepay only as a value offer with easy exit.
- Productise one or two maintenance or next-version add-ons. Measure their impact monthly so the case makes itself.
- Project first, retainer second: return-framed updates during the project, the pitch at delivery when the win is fresh, handover documents that seed the next scope.
- Gate every client-specific extra with four questions. Who takes the late-night call in six months? Is it right for the product, or only for this client? Who owns it when you leave? What breaks? Ship it like it runs for eighteen months, because it will.
- Enterprise form: land on the measurable cost-saver, expand into the workflows the delivered system makes possible, success defined in writing on the first call.
- The live ask, the objection and the price are `discovery-to-close`.

## Procedure: referrals

- Design them in from day one. Track source from the first visit; "word of mouth" is not an attribution answer.
- Reward both sides in product value, not cash. Cash commissions are a different job.
- Ask at the happiest moment: the review where the numbers are on screen, or the month-one win. Be specific about who to think of and what to watch for. Give them a forwardable paragraph.
- Ask for one introduction at the end of every engagement. One sentence.
- Aim at the people who already love the work.
- Reviews: ask after the buyer has had the value. Log eligible buyers, requests made and reviews landed. Never pay for praise.
- A testimonial opens on the doubt. A metrics-only case study when anonymity is needed.

## Persuasion in its honest form only

A real gift, not hidden debt. Real scarcity, not invented. True specific praise, not flattery. The client's real loss, not a manufactured one. Written success criteria in their words. One named owner per action in a committee. Never change terms after commitment; reopen the decision instead.

## Learning from your own record

Re-read your own won and lost threads. Compare the best mails to the worst, the winning calls to the losing ones. The pattern that predicts your wins is often not the one you would guess. The vault already holds the threads; make it a scheduled read.

When a former client's numbers dip later, re-open without ego: how is it going; want me to take a look.

## Checks

- Every account has a written success definition and dated checkpoints.
- Every review recap used real numbers or named the instrument to start.
- Every renewal has its review and talk scheduled months out.
- Every expansion proposed names one observed gap.
- Every referral ask names a who and carries a forwardable blurb.

## You slip here

- A discount to save a renewal instead of proof.
- "Anything else I can help with?"
- A fabricated return figure in a review.
- Tapering on a paying client's silence.
- Selling continuity you cannot staff.
