# Call plans

Timed instruments. Mark core and optional. Cut what does not fit. The lines are shapes; say them your way.

## Discovery, 25 minutes

| min | part | core? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | show prep in one line; confirm the time; ask permission to record | core |
| 2 to 4 | "What made you take this call?" (booked) or "What are you trying to solve, and what have you tried?" (other) | core |
| 4 to 12 | peel to impact: problem, cause, what it blocks, the metric, the executive consequence | core |
| 12 to 15 | the three areas; let them pick; three sub-problems | core |
| 15 to 18 | "When did you realise it had to be fixed?" The bill, the incident. | core |
| 18 to 20 | must-solve or nice-to-have; who else is involved; what a fix is worth to them | core |
| 20 to 22 | "What would you hope to see if we showed you something?" | optional |
| 22 to 25 | the success metric in writing; the next step with two slots | core |

Problem buckets, pre-written, three or four: {{bucket 1}}, {{bucket 2}}, {{bucket 3}}. A prospect who fits none is a positioning signal.

Questions that keep it about them:

- "Walk me through the last time that happened."
- "What have you tried already that didn't work?"
- "How are you handling it now, and what does that cost in time and money?"
- "Where does the budget for this come from?"
- "Who else is losing sleep over this?"
- "What should I have asked that I haven't?"

Anchor generic answers: "usually," "always," "we'd probably" get "when did that last happen? Talk me through it."

Dig at emotion: "that seems to really bother you; I bet there's a story."

## Demo, 30 minutes

| min | part |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 | the story: the problem, who has it, the cost, why current fixes fail, what changed |
| 3 to 5 | "Before we go in, is this your first look, or do you know us?" |
| 5 to 20 | their data, their workflow, their wishlist callbacks; before and after in their job |
| 20 to 25 | their questions; label a feature question first ("sounds like X matters; what's behind that?") then show |
| 25 to 30 | the trial close; the next step booked on the call |

Mid-call disqualify line: "From what we've covered, I don't think we'd be much use to you. We mostly help {{type}}. I'll send materials and give you half an hour back."

## Proposal review, 20 minutes

| min | part |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | value alignment: the problems they named, the fix for each, "aligned, apart from price?" |
| 5 to 8 | vendor of choice: "of the options, are we the pick, apart from price?" |
| 8 to 12 | process: "if price works, walk me through go-live, approvals, legal" |
| 12 to 14 | the three options, the price, silence |
| 14 to 20 | their response; the full ask; trades; the close date |

## The one-page negotiation prep

Write it before any call where price, scope or conflict is live.

1. **Goal.** The specific best case, written, sanity-checked with someone.
2. **Summary.** The known facts in two or three sentences that could earn "that's right."
3. **Labels and the audit.** Three to five things they might think or say about you, the unfair ones included, to say first.
4. **Questions.** Three to five open questions, including the deal-killer and the behind-the-table question.
5. **Non-cash list.** What they own that is cheap for them and valuable to you: a case study, referrals, prepay, a longer term, a logo, implementation help.

Make approvals look like effort. A reply four hours later lands better than an instant yes.

## The champion's one-page case

In their company's words. Problem. Approach. Payoff. Investment. Return and payback. A forwardable artifact the champion presents as their own win.

## Price framing lines

- Tie it to their economics: "{{price}} a month is {{a fraction of the thing it saves}}; it saves {{hours or units}} a week."
- A stated return ratio: "at your volume we'd expect {{outcome}} worth {{their figure}}, against {{price}}."
- Never apologise for a price. Say what it buys.

## Closing lines

- Trial close: "Given all this, do you see it solving {{their problem}}?"
- The ask: "Is there anything preventing us from getting you started?"
- Verbal yes: "I'll send the e-sign after this call and you can sign today. Right?"
- Legal time-box: "If it closes inside this window you keep the incentive; past it, it goes. Shall we book a follow-up this week?"
