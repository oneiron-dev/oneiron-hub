# X direct messages

Checked 2026-10-21 from retained copies of X's pages (limits captured 2026-09-26; automation rules captured 2026-10-12, page updated April 2026). No account was logged in and no allowance was tested. Every default here is one the owner can override; the override line records who and why.

## What X publishes

- 500 DMs a day as a technical account limit, counted across devices and apps. X says limits can be reduced. A time-based limit returns an error and a wait.
- The automation rules say not to send unsolicited messages. Non-API website scripting may result in permanent suspension.
- An account owner's OAuth permission authorises the app. It says nothing about the recipient's consent.

The 500 figure is the top of a technical range. It is not an allowance for 500 cold conversations. Account state, subscription and API entitlements can lower actual access.

## Loop default

A small number of manual, context-relevant conversations. Open after public interaction or an invitation to DM. No mass unsolicited automated DMs. No cold-DM-a-day number is invented, because the sources hold none. Public interaction is a relevance cue, not consent to a bot sequence.

Practitioners on X promote open-tracking systems and pre-warmed multi-inbox stacks. `../practice/folklore.md` weighs them.

## The law on an X DM

A private X message is electronic mail under UK PECR and the EU rule. Read the law file's section "LinkedIn and other private social DMs" for the recipient's country. In the US, give it CAN-SPAM content: identification, an opt-out, a postal address.

## If you override

More outreach may open more conversations. Duplicate unsolicited pitches can be reported, and filtering, suspension or API restriction follow. Premium does not change the automation rules. The legal exposure follows the country file.
