# Australia: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Consent or real inferred consent for electronic outreach, including B2B; phone is a separate DNCR regime.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Express consent or a documented, narrow inferred-consent case; conspicuous publication is not universal permission.
- **B2C default:** Express consent or genuinely evidenced expected ongoing-relationship inference.
- **Default channel basis:** SpamAct express/inferred consent; Australian-link commercial electronic message.
- **Data-processing basis:** PrivacyAct/APPs where applicable; no automatic public-data exemption.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Commercial email, SMS/MMS and instant messages with an Australian link generally require consent; B2B is not categorically exempt. Inference from conspicuously published business details requires no contrary statement and direct relevance to role/functions; exact Schedule1 wording was not retrieved. Asking for consent in a commercial electronic message is itself regulated.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Accurate sender/authorising business identity and contact details, kept correct at least 30 days. Clear functional unsubscribe; no extra fee, extra personal data, login or account creation. Unsubscribe facility available for at least 30 days.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Within5 working days; loop suppresses immediately.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** ACMA has imposed seven-figure penalties for large campaigns; lack of consent and broken opt-outs are separate issues. A public-address inference needs proof, not a vendor assertion.
- **Evidence:** [AU-spam]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** Social feature must actually be a directed electronic/instant message; public advertising differs.
- **Note:** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED for exact conspicuous-publication test, harvested-address prohibition text and current2026 branded-SMS sender-ID registration scope/commencement.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Relevant manually dialled genuine business number after internal objection checks and applicable calling standards.
- **B2C default:** Do Not Call Register wash/current valid consent or exemption, plus calling standards.
- **Default channel basis:** DoNotCallRegisterAct; business-number eligibility differs from personal-use numbers.
- **Data-processing basis:** APP7 where PrivacyAct applies.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** The register generally covers eligible private/domestic numbers, not genuine business-only numbers; a proprietor’s mobile may be eligible. Industry telemarketing standards can apply even to exempt callers or unlisted numbers. This detailed statutory split is not fully quoted in the retrieved overview.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Caller/principal name, purpose, contact details and lawful caller ID. Observe current telemarketing local-time rules; exact windows to verify before scheduling.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** End call and suppress on request; do not confuse register wash periods with electronic-message5workingday unsubscribe.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Number-type and exemption mistakes can cause DNCR enforcement; ignoring a direct objection aggravates data/marketing risk.
- **Evidence:** [AU-dnc], [AU-spam]. Status: `primary_quote_unverified`.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Consent by default plus applicable DNCR/industry standards; automation is not a general SpamAct voice-call rule.
- **B2C default:** Same default.
- **Default channel basis:** DNCR and telemarketing industry standards; actual automated campaign requirements to verify.
- **Data-processing basis:** PrivacyAct/APPs where applicable.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Voice calls are not the SpamAct electronic-message regime. Automated telemarketing still requires the applicable DNCR and calling-standard compliance; a universal separate Australian robocall opt-in law is NOT asserted here.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Required caller identity, purpose, contact/caller ID and applicable termination mechanism; exact current standard to confirm.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Cease on objection; exact automated-call rule not primary-quoted.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** DNCR/calling-standard action and source-data duties; do not import TCPA dollar damages into Australia.
- **Evidence:** [AU-dnc]. Status: `primary_quote_unverified`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

Where the PrivacyAct applies, check APP3 collection/necessity/fairness, APP5 notice, APP7 direct-marketing conditions and source disclosure/opt-out, APP8 transfers and APP11 security; small-business exemption has exceptions, including some trading-in-personal-information activities. SpamAct bans relevant address-harvesting software/harvested-list use; a purchased list is not evidence of consent. Exact statute/APP texts were not quoted here: PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED. [AU-spam]

Sources: [AU-spam], [AU-dnc], [AU-woolworths].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

SpamAct civil penalties depend on penalty units, corporate multiplier, repeat conduct and daily caps. Current2026 numeric ceiling was not verified; do not reuse a2020 dollar maximum. Actual Woolworths amount is a verification lead, not confirmed from this retrieval.

- **Woolworths, ACMA July 2020 — AUD1,003,200 reported infringement notice.** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED: ACMA case page returned503. Reported more than5m messages after opt-out; bulk compliance failure, not the typical cost of one B2B email. Sources: [AU-woolworths].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
