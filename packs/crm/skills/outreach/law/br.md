# Brazil: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** LGPD legitimate interests can support proportionate outreach; telecom/state restrictions are separate, not a universal national email opt-in.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Relevant, limited professional outreach after documented LGPD legitimate-interest balancing and source/privacy notice; consent if that assessment fails.
- **B2C default:** Consent default; a legitimate-interest route is possible but not automatic and needs context/expectation balancing.
- **Default channel basis:** No CASL-style general prior-consent email/DM/SMS statute verified here; sector/consumer/state rules can differ.
- **Data-processing basis:** LGPD7(IX)/10 legitimate interests or valid consent; special-category data needs separate grounds.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** LGPD permits legitimate interests for legitimate concrete purposes, subject to fundamental rights and necessity. That is a data-processing basis, not an absolute marketing exemption. Do not assert that every Brazilian marketing message requires consent or that public numbers allow unlimited SMS.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Controller/company identity, purpose, contact and privacy/source information required by applicable LGPD transparency duties. Simple free stop/objection route is the default; real postal footer recommended for email, not claimed universally mandatory.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Cease marketing on objection by recommendation; process statutory rights under the applicable basis. No universal10-day marketing unsubscribe deadline verified; LGPD15-day detailed-access response is a different right.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** ANPD orders/fines and civil/consumer claims can follow unlawful collection/use; liability depends on actual processing, not just sending channel.
- **Evidence:** [BR-camara]. Status: `mixed`.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Relevant professional calls only after lawful-data assessment, internal/sector/state DNC checks and current Anatel caller-ID/numbering rules; consent recommended for automated calling.
- **B2C default:** Consent default; if choosing an opt-out route, check NãoMePerturbe coverage, state Procon lists and current call rules.
- **Default channel basis:** Anatel telecom/numbering rules, sector/state DNC and consumer law; no single nationwide all-sector opt-in asserted.
- **Data-processing basis:** LGPD legitimate interests after balancing or consent.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Anatel’s updated page says NãoMePerturbe blocks telecom-operator and payroll-loan-bank telemarketing, with exceptions; it is not an all-industry national DNC. Anatel blocks excessive short calls and requires authentication for large callers. Current0303/other prefix exceptions and state rules need primary confirmation before calling.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Real caller/principal identity and purpose, lawful caller-ID/authentication/numbering and usable objection route.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** End/suppress on request by recommendation; exact state/sector processing periods must be checked.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Telecom-resource blocking, consumer enforcement and LGPD liability; dialler traffic/short-call patterns create operational exposure beyond a single manual B2B call.
- **Evidence:** [BR-calls], [BR-camara]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** Current Anatel page: large-caller authentication over 500,000 calls/month; this is not a universal safe-volume allowance.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

LGPD7(IX),10: concrete legitimate purpose, necessity and balancing; only strictly necessary personal data, transparency and safeguards.7§§3–4 and related provisions limit reuse of publicly accessible or manifestly public information; publication does not cancel purpose/good-faith/rights requirements. Enrichment and sharing need their own compatible basis, security/retention, controller/processor allocation and international-transfer mechanism. Quote source is official2018 original text; current ANPD guidance/regulations and amendments need confirmation. [BR-camara]

Sources: [BR-camara], [BR-calls], [BR-anpd-cases].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

LGPD52 original text: fine up to 2% of private legal entity/group/conglomerate Brazilian prior-year turnover, excluding taxes, cappedBRL50m per infringement. This is not an automatic fine on a natural person for each email; statutory scope/sanction procedure matters. Warnings, blocking/deletion, consumer and civil remedies also exist.

- **Telekall Infoservice, ANPD first fine July 2023 — BRL14,400 reported, twoBRL7,200 fines.** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED: ANPD release restricted401. Reported small-company unlawful telemarketing-data processing/cooperation case; more relevant to list operators than unrelated giant platform fines. Sources: [BR-anpd-cases].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
