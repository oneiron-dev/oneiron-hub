# Spain: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Opt-in electronic outreach, but manual calls have a narrower alternative lawful-basis route.

Read the EU baseline too: `eu.md`. A GDPR lawful basis and channel permission are separate.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Prior request/express authorisation; no broad professional-role or public-address exception.
- **B2C default:** Same; own-customer similar-product exception only under LSSI21.2.
- **Default channel basis:** LSSI21 electronic mail or equivalent electronic means.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis; legitimate interests do not override LSSI.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Art.21 prohibits unsolicited electronic commercial communication unless previously requested or expressly authorised. Its prior-contract similar-product exception is not a purchased-prospect list. Treat private promotional social messages as equivalent electronic outreach by default; exact platform classification is an interpretation.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details. Commercial nature and identity must be clearly identifiable (LSSI20).
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Easy free objection in every send; cease on objection. No imported US ten-business-day allowance.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Even B2B outreach can breach LSSI; separate GDPR issues can involve the broker, enrichment and overseas processors.
- **Evidence:** [ES-lssi], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Genuine role-relevant professional call only with a documented Art.6 basis and current AEPD conditions; otherwise consent.
- **B2C default:** Consent by default; an assessed legitimate-interest route is not excluded by statute but requires its actual conditions.
- **Default channel basis:** LGT66.1(b): consent OR another valid GDPR6.1 ground; AEPD Circular1/2023 interpretation needs review.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Spain does not categorically require consent for every manual marketing call:66.1(b) expressly allows another Art.6.1 basis. No inference that a random purchased consumer number has legitimate-interest permission. Robinson exclusion screening and 2025 numbering restrictions require current confirmation.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify trader, commercial purpose and objection/withdrawal route at start; caller ID/current authorised numbering.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Immediate suppression on objection; exact circular/numbering details unquoted.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Overbroad use of legitimate interest can attract unwanted-call and data-law complaints; Spain has substantial campaign enforcement.
- **Evidence:** [ES-lgt], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED for detailed Circular1/2023 professional-call presumption and current numbering restrictions.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Prior consent.
- **B2C default:** Prior consent.
- **Default channel basis:** LGT66.1(a).
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Automatic calls without human intervention require prior consent; the live-call alternate-lawful-basis branch does not replace that.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on withdrawal/objection.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Automation is a distinct consent-based rule even if a manual professional call would qualify.
- **Evidence:** [ES-lgt]. Status: `mixed`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

For identifiable people, document GDPR Art. 6 consent or a purpose/necessity/balancing legitimate-interest assessment where appropriate. Recital 47 is not automatic permission. Art. 14: disclose identity, purposes, basis, source (including whether public), recipients, retention, rights and transfers within one month, or at first contact/disclosure if earlier. Art. 21: stop direct-marketing processing on objection. Minimise fields, verify provenance, agree Art. 28 processor terms, secure data and meet Chapter V transfer rules. Public profiles, enrichment and purchased lists are not exemptions; special-category data needs Art. 9 grounds. Channel consent remains a separate question. [EU-gdpr-original]

Sources: [ES-lssi], [ES-lgt], [ES-vodafone], [EU-gdpr-original].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

LSSI39 distinguishes minor/serious/very serious sanctions: up to€30,000; €30,001–150,000; €150,001–600,000 respectively. Categorisation and current consolidated text matter. GDPR and telecom penalties are separate.

- **Vodafone España, AEPD PS/00059/2020, resolution published2021 — €8.15m original total: €4m + €2m GDPR, €150k LSSI, €2m telecom.** Original primary resolution retrieved; later appeals/reductions/payment NOT checked. Large sustained multi-channel outsourced campaigns and governance failures; €8.15m is not a per-email fine. Sources: [ES-vodafone].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
