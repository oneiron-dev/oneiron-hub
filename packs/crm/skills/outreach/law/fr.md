# France: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Role-related professional email is an important exception; consumer calling changed in 2026 and needs primary confirmation.

Read the EU baseline too: `eu.md`. A GDPR lawful basis and channel permission are separate.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Professional-role relevant opt-out outreach only when the CNIL professional-recipient conditions actually fit; prefer consent for SMS/ambiguous social accounts.
- **B2C default:** Prior consent or properly documented same-seller similar-product customer exception.
- **Default channel basis:** CPCE L34-5; CNIL professional-prospecting interpretation (primary quote unavailable).
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR legitimate interests for permitted relevant professional prospecting; otherwise consent.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** French professional electronic prospecting is commonly permitted with information and easy objection when the subject relates to the person’s profession; this is not every B2B sale to any employee. Primary CNIL pages were in maintenance, so exact current language and each medium’s scope are unquoted.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Prompt cessation after objection; no verified French 10-day grace. GDPR Art.21 applies.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Role mismatch, private numbers/accounts and hidden data provenance can remove the professional route. GDPR and French communications/consumer-law remedies apply.
- **Evidence:** [FR-email], [FR-cnil-alt], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `primary_quote_unverified`.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Relevant manually dialled genuine professional calls after objection screening; verify current scope.
- **B2C default:** Consent by default for post-11 August 2026 consumer calls; confirm current law/exception before using an existing-contract branch.
- **Default channel basis:** French 2026 consumer telephone opt-in reform; exact current primary text not retrieved.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent / legitimate interests where calling law allows.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED: the reported change effective 11 August 2026 replaces the legacy Bloctel-only consumer approach with consent, subject to exceptions. Do not ship an older Bloctel-only consumer default. Professional and sector-specific scope needs confirmation.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Caller/principal identity, commercial purpose, lawful caller ID and objection route; exact new disclosures unverified.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on objection; verify new statutory consent/contract exceptions and any required record retention.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Using an obsolete consumer opt-out model after the reported reform risks consumer-law enforcement; specific current fines must be checked.
- **Evidence:** [FR-phone], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `primary_quote_unverified`.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Specific prior consent by default.
- **B2C default:** Specific prior consent.
- **Default channel basis:** CPCE L34-5 / ePrivacy automated communication rule; corporate scope to confirm.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Do not use professional-email practice to infer permission for recorded/artificial-voice calling; exact French automation and legal-person text is not quoted here.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Cease on withdrawal/objection.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Automation removes the rationale for treating the interaction as an ordinary screened professional call.
- **Evidence:** [FR-email], [EU-privacy-ukcopy]. Status: `primary_quote_unverified`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

For identifiable people, document GDPR Art. 6 consent or a purpose/necessity/balancing legitimate-interest assessment where appropriate. Recital 47 is not automatic permission. Art. 14: disclose identity, purposes, basis, source (including whether public), recipients, retention, rights and transfers within one month, or at first contact/disclosure if earlier. Art. 21: stop direct-marketing processing on objection. Minimise fields, verify provenance, agree Art. 28 processor terms, secure data and meet Chapter V transfer rules. Public profiles, enrichment and purchased lists are not exemptions; special-category data needs Art. 9 grounds. Channel consent remains a separate question. [EU-gdpr-original] CNIL Kaspr is a directly relevant enrichment lead: restricted visibility settings, source transparency, retention and transfer duties need attention [FR-kaspr].

Sources: [FR-email], [FR-cnil-alt], [FR-phone], [FR-kaspr], [EU-gdpr-original], [EU-privacy-ukcopy].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

GDPR ceilings apply to data-law breaches. CPCE/consumer-law fines are additional national routes; current 2026 telephone ceilings and exact L34-5 sanction provisions were not quoted and must be verified, not guessed.

- **Kaspr, CNIL 5 December 2024 — €240,000 reported.** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED: CNIL returned HTTP503 maintenance. Lead-enrichment/LinkedIn scraping enforcement, not a finding that all public business-address collection is illegal. Sources: [FR-kaspr].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
