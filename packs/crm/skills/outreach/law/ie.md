# Ireland: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** A real professional-email exception; mobiles and ambiguous DMs are not treated as work email.

Read the EU baseline too: `eu.md`. A GDPR lawful basis and channel permission are separate.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Role-relevant opt-out email to an address reasonably appearing mainly commercial/official, or corporate recipient; document that judgment.
- **B2C default:** Consent; own-customer similar-product exception only with collection and each-message opt-out plus Irish 12-month condition.
- **Default channel basis:** SI336/2011 reg.13(2) business-address exception / reg.13(4) legal-person opt-out; otherwise consent.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR legitimate interests after assessment for permitted professional email.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Ireland expressly excludes an email sent to a mainly commercial/official address, solely about that activity, from reg.13(1). This is not permission to message any private account of an employee.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** No fixed electronic-message N-day grace identified; stop on objection; GDPR Art.21.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Natural-person non-business email without consent can be a separate criminal offence per message. Business exception does not waive objections or transparency.
- **Evidence:** [IE-law], [IE-guidance], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.

### LinkedIn and other private social DMs

- **B2B default:** Consent unless the exact business electronic-address exception and account subscriber facts are confirmed; do not generalise from work email.
- **B2C default:** Consent; narrowly checked customer exception.
- **Default channel basis:** Irish electronic-mail consent rule; business-email exception extension to this medium not verified.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent / assessed lawful basis.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** The statute’s electronic-mail definition is broad, but reg.13(2) specifically describes an email address. Whether a particular LinkedIn account or number satisfies it needs confirmation.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on objection; no general 10-business-day allowance established.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Treating every business social account/mobile as exempt risks an unsupported exception and per-message offences.
- **Evidence:** [IE-law], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `primary_quote_unverified`.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Screen NDD/internal objections for fixed business lines; obtain consent for mobiles.
- **B2C default:** Fixed-line opt-out after NDD screen; mobiles require consent.
- **Default channel basis:** Reg.13(5) fixed-line opt-out; reg.13(6) mobile consent.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** The mobile telephone rule is stricter than the general landline rule and is not safely replaced by “B2B”.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Caller and principal name; valid reachable contact details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on objection. NDD 28-day recording rule is not permission to ignore a direct objection.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** A mobile dialled manually still has the Irish mobile-consent issue.
- **Evidence:** [IE-law], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Specific consent by default; corporate fixed-line reg.13(3) opt-out exception requires NDD/objection screening if chosen.
- **B2C default:** Consent.
- **Default channel basis:** Reg.13(1),(3),(6).
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Natural persons require consent for automated calls; legal-person fixed lines have a statutory opt-out branch; mobiles require consent. Default consent is stricter than that corporate branch.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Caller/principal name, address and telephone number.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on withdrawal/objection.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Using the corporate branch without verifying fixed-line legal-person status risks criminal offences.
- **Evidence:** [IE-law]. Status: `mixed`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

For identifiable people, document GDPR Art. 6 consent or a purpose/necessity/balancing legitimate-interest assessment where appropriate. Recital 47 is not automatic permission. Art. 14: disclose identity, purposes, basis, source (including whether public), recipients, retention, rights and transfers within one month, or at first contact/disclosure if earlier. Art. 21: stop direct-marketing processing on objection. Minimise fields, verify provenance, agree Art. 28 processor terms, secure data and meet Chapter V transfer rules. Public profiles, enrichment and purchased lists are not exemptions; special-category data needs Art. 9 grounds. Channel consent remains a separate question. [EU-gdpr-original]

Sources: [IE-law], [IE-guidance], [IE-report2025], [EU-gdpr-original].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

Reg.13: each unsolicited message/call can be an offence. Enacted text specifies class A summary fine, or on indictment up to €250,000 corporate / €50,000 natural person. Confirm amendments and current summary fine value. GDPR ceilings separately apply to data breaches.

- **Irish DPC 2025 electronic direct-marketing complaints — 275 investigations/complaints concluded;50 warning letters (not monetary fines).** Annual report checked; a named marketing conviction and fine amount remain unverified. DPC2025 report, pp.28–29:245 new complaints,275 investigations concluded and50 warning letters. Shows actual complaint handling, but no verified named conviction/fine amount here. Sources: [IE-report2025].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
