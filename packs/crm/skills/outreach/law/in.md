# India: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Separate telecom consent/preferences and registration from ordinary email/DM; DPDP operative dates are a major verification item.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Relevant public-role B2B contact with transparent identity/source and immediate opt-out by recommendation; confirm operative data law first.
- **B2C default:** Consent recommended for consumer marketing; do not invent a telecom/DLT mandate for ordinary email or app DM.
- **Default channel basis:** No general email/social prior-consent statute established in this research; TCCCPR is not automatically an email/app-DM regime.
- **Data-processing basis:** Operative IT/privacy/sector law; DPDP commencement and transitional duties need primary confirmation.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** TRAI TCCCPR addresses commercial telecom voice/SMS. This is not evidence that ordinary email/LinkedIn DMs require DLT registration or that public-data scraping is unrestricted. DPDP Act/Rules phase-in and current obligations were not primary-quoted; a2023 enactment date alone is not commencement.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Truthful sender/company, marketing purpose, usable reply/stop and appropriate source/privacy notice (loop defaults, not a claimed universal Indian CAN-SPAM footer).
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Immediate suppression by recommendation; no universal email/DM10-day statutory deadline verified.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Possible privacy, consumer, sector and contract/account remedies; exposure depends on operative law and source. Do not use an uncommenced maximum as a current per-email penalty.
- **Evidence:** [IN-sender], [IN-dpdp-official]. Status: `primary_quote_unverified`.

### SMS

- **B2B default:** Use registered commercial telecom routes with PE/telemarketer, templates/headers as applicable and documented consent or lawful customer-preference permission; no general B2B exemption.
- **B2C default:** Same route; consent default. Preference-permitted promotional traffic may be possible without express consent under current category rules.
- **Default channel basis:** TRAI TCCCPR/DLT consent-or-preference model, registered sender/telemarketer; current amendments apply.
- **Data-processing basis:** Operative privacy/sector law; DPDP phase-in to confirm.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** The official2018 regulations define promotional communications as those without explicit consent, so “all Indian promotional traffic always requires express consent” is too broad. Preferences, category/time controls and registration matter. Sender guidance requires PE registration, header/content/consent templates as applicable and delivery identifiers for bulk messages. Voice numbering and autodialler declarations need current directions; do not use ordinary personal SIMs as a commercial-send workaround.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** SMS: registered PE, appropriate registered header/template and prescribed delivery identifiers; current URL/callback whitelisting to confirm. Calls: caller/principal/purpose, correct commercial number series and any prescribed automatic-call disclosure;140promotional/1600service-transactional rollout and scope need current verification.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Use consent-revocation/preference mechanisms; suppress immediately by recommendation. Exact current DLT processing/complaint deadlines not verified.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Carrier rejection, telecom-resource disconnection/blacklisting and regulatory financial disincentives. A provider’s statutory disincentive is not automatically the founder’s per-message fine.
- **Evidence:** [IN-sender], [IN-tcccpr], [IN-trai], [IN-ucc-measures]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** Automated live-agent dialling versus artificial/prerecorded voice may have different declarations/obligations; confirm current2025/26 directions.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

Use documented source, purpose, minimisation, retention/security and prompt suppression as loop recommendations. Do not scrape sensitive/private details or infer telecom consent from a business directory. The DPDP publicly-available-data exclusion is narrowly framed around publication by the data principal or a legally obliged person; operative text/commencement and application to enriched/broker-republished records need confirmation. The reported2025 Rules/18-month phase-in means core duties may not yet be operative inOctober 2026; no exact commencement date is asserted without the Gazette. Existing IT/SPDI, sector, consumer and contractual rules still need analysis. [IN-dpdp-official: PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED; IN-tcccpr]

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

Practical telecom exposure includes rejected delivery, disconnection and blacklisting. Exact current2025/26 financial-disincentive schedules and sender liability are unquoted. DPDP’s reported up-toINR250crore security-breach maximum is NOT a universal cold-outreach fine and must not be applied before relevant commencement; primary penalty text remains to verify.

- **TRAI UCC enforcement and financial-disincentive records — No named imposed fine amount verified.** OPEN: sender/FAQ confirms disconnection/blacklisting powers, not a named completed action or fine. Do not invent a telco-wide rupee aggregate or treat a carrier disincentive as a personal per-text penalty. Sources: [IN-trai], [IN-financial].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
