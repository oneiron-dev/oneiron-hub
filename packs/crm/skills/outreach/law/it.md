# Italy: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Consent for electronic marketing; live calls need lawful sourcing and opposition-register handling.

Read the EU baseline too: `eu.md`. A GDPR lawful basis and channel permission are separate.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Consent. Do not infer it from public business addresses or professional networks.
- **B2C default:** Consent; own-customer soft-spam exception for email only if Art.130(4) conditions fit.
- **Default channel basis:** PrivacyCode130(1),(2); email exception130(4).
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent / lawful basis for independent list operations.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Art.130(2) extends the consent rule to email, MMS, SMS or other electronic messages. The same-seller similar-product soft-spam branch is specifically for electronic-mail coordinates obtained in a sale, not automatically SMS or DMs.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop after objection; no verified general N-day grace.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Italian B2B is not a general opt-out email market. Scraped business contact details do not supply channel consent.
- **Evidence:** [IT-privacycode], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Use only a lawful telephone source/basis and current RPO screening; unregistered does not itself license an unlawfully collected number.
- **B2C default:** Consent by default unless the lawful source and opt-out/RPO route are evidenced.
- **Default channel basis:** PrivacyCode130(3),(3bis) and expanded RPO rules.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** RPO covers fixed/mobile numbers under expanded rules; registration revokes relevant earlier telemarketing consents subject to statutory exceptions. Operators must check lists before campaigns and at statutory intervals; exact current exemptions and intervals need a source refresh.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify operator/principal and commercial purpose; lawful displayed caller ID and opposition instructions.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** End/suppress on objection; distinguish RPO processing/list-validity intervals from permission to keep calling.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Large Italian telemarketing penalties focus on consent, uncontrolled call-centre chains and repeated objections.
- **Evidence:** [IT-privacycode], [IT-rpo], [IT-tim]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED for detailed current RPO time windows and exceptions.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Specific prior consent and RPO effects checked.
- **B2C default:** Same.
- **Default channel basis:** PrivacyCode130(1).
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Automated advertising calls without an operator require consent; a live-call directory/RPO route is not a substitute.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Cease on withdrawal; apply RPO consent revocation where relevant.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Clear consent issue plus separate data-law exposure; employing an intermediary does not remove controller responsibilities.
- **Evidence:** [IT-privacycode], [IT-rpo]. Status: `mixed`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

For identifiable people, document GDPR Art. 6 consent or a purpose/necessity/balancing legitimate-interest assessment where appropriate. Recital 47 is not automatic permission. Art. 14: disclose identity, purposes, basis, source (including whether public), recipients, retention, rights and transfers within one month, or at first contact/disclosure if earlier. Art. 21: stop direct-marketing processing on objection. Minimise fields, verify provenance, agree Art. 28 processor terms, secure data and meet Chapter V transfer rules. Public profiles, enrichment and purchased lists are not exemptions; special-category data needs Art. 9 grounds. Channel consent remains a separate question. [EU-gdpr-original]

Sources: [IT-privacycode], [IT-rpo], [IT-tim], [EU-gdpr-original].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

GDPR sanctions reach the relevant Art.83 ceiling where applicable; Italian channel and opposition-register duties also apply. Do not treat the TIM total as routine exposure or double-count it under each channel.

- **TIM S.p.A., Garante15 January 2020 — €27,802,946 original penalty order.** Primary order retrieved; appeal/payment/any later reduction not checked. Order mentions half-payment settlement option, not proof of actual payment. Systemic large-scale consent, marketing, governance and other GDPR violations; not a fine per cold approach. Sources: [IT-tim].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
