# South Korea: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Express consent for electronic advertising, including B2B; a specific telephone-sales source-disclosure exception exists.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Express prior marketing consent; no general business/public-profile exception.
- **B2C default:** Same default; direct same-kind customer exception only if current statutory/decree conditions fit.
- **Default channel basis:** NetworkAct50(1) express consent or narrow direct-transaction exception.
- **Data-processing basis:** PIPA consent or another actual statutory ground; legitimate interest does not override channel consent.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** NetworkAct50 requires express prior consent for for-profit advertising by electronic transmission medium. Directly collected transactional contacts for same-kind goods/services have a limited decree-period exception. Exact6-month term and social-feature coverage need current confirmation; prefer consent.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Sender name/contact and easy free refusal/consent-withdrawal method. Korean advertising label(광고), prescribed placement, toll-free refusal and message-format details require current decree/KISA confirmation; default include the label, do not call every detail primary-verified. No deceptive identity or automatic generation/registration of recipient addresses.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** No further advertising after refusal/withdrawal; default immediate. Confirmation/outcome notice and periodic consent-check deadlines need current decree verification.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** NetworkAct76 original translated text provides up to KRW30m administrative fine for specified50(1)–(4) violations. Separate PIPA penalties/claims and carrier blocking can apply.
- **Evidence:** [KR-law2], [KR-pipa-full]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** Extra consent for21:00–08:00 transmissions is the statutory baseline, but some media have decree exceptions; do not falsely apply night-consent rule universally to email. Default send08:00–21:00 recipient local time.
- **Note:** Regular consent confirmation is required by statute; reported2-year confirmation/14-day outcome deadlines remain PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Consent by default; a properly qualified telemarketer branch may permit cold voice solicitation with source disclosure.
- **B2C default:** Same default; check telephone-sales DNC and registration/consumer rules.
- **Default channel basis:** NetworkAct50(1)(2) telemarketer voice source-disclosure exception; separate consumer-law duties.
- **Data-processing basis:** PIPA actual lawful basis and source disclosure.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** 50(1)(2) expressly exempts a telemarketer under the Door-to-Door Sales Act who informs prospective customers by voice of their personal-information collection source and solicits by telephone. This does not legalise an unlawfully sourced list or remove later refusal/DNC duties.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Caller/principal, commercial purpose, source of personal information for the exception and contact/refusal method.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Cease after refusal/withdrawal; confirm current statutory DNC/consumer rules.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Using the telemarketer branch without fitting registration/source/consumer conditions creates consent and privacy exposure.
- **Evidence:** [KR-law2], [KR-pipa-full]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** Do not present Korea as consent-only for every possible live call; default consent is a recommendation stricter than the quoted exception.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Express prior consent by default; do not assume a recorded/AI pitch qualifies for human telephone-sales source exception.
- **B2C default:** Same.
- **Default channel basis:** NetworkAct50 electronic advertising; exact voice-exception scope to verify.
- **Data-processing basis:** PIPA.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** A broad electronic-medium consent rule applies; applicability of the telephone-sales exception to a particular artificial/recorded service must be confirmed, not assumed.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on withdrawal/refusal;08:00–21:00 default unless valid separate night consent and applicable medium rules.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Consent/format/night rules, consumer telemarketing law and source-data penalties can coexist.
- **Evidence:** [KR-law2], [KR-pipa-full]. Status: `mixed`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

PIPA15 allows specified grounds including legitimate interests only where manifestly superior and substantially related/reasonable; not a general GDPR-equivalent marketing pass. Articles17–18,20,21,26/28-8 and related rules govern third-party purpose/use, sources/notification, deletion, outsourcing and transfers. Public profiles and purchased enrichment are not an express advertising opt-in. PIPA current source/notification duties may differ by controller size and trigger. Translation/version check is mandatory before implementation. [KR-pipa-full; KR-law2]

Sources: [KR-law2], [KR-pipa-full], [KR-meta-case].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

NetworkAct official translated2020-era text: up to KRW30m for specified advertising consent/refusal/content breaches. Serious address manipulation/other conduct can have different sanctions. PIPA current turnover-based penalty provisions were not verified here and must not be substituted with GDPR4%.

- **Google and Meta, PIPC14 September 2022 — KRW69.2bn Google andKRW30.8bn Meta.** Primary release retrieved; PIPC search also shows subsequent litigation outcomes, which were not fully reviewed. Behavioural-data collection/use for personalised advertising, not a cold-message fine. Do not use this scale as a per-SMS estimate. Sources: [KR-meta-case].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
