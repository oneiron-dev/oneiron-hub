# Netherlands: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Business email generally needs consent; only expressly designated published contacts qualify for the statutory public-address route.

Read the EU baseline too: `eu.md`. A GDPR lawful basis and channel permission are separate.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Consent; or an electronic contact expressly designated/published for unsolicited communications of this kind, used for that stated purpose.
- **B2C default:** Consent or narrow same-seller similar-product customer exception.
- **Default channel basis:** Telecommunicatiewet11.7(1),(3),(4).
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent / assessed legitimate interests where send exception applies.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Dutch electronic-message opt-in applies to businesses too. Art.11.7(3)(a) requires designation/publication for unsolicited commercial/charitable/ideal communications, not merely a visible contact address. Customer exception is distinct. A business social profile alone does not satisfy designation.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details. Art.11.7(7) requires actual sender identity and a valid postadres or number for cessation requests; retain exact Dutch wording for interpretation.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on objection; no fixed Dutch N-day sending grace verified.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** A bare “info@” scrape or role relevance does not establish the designated-address exception. ACM sanctions and civil/data-law remedies can apply.
- **Evidence:** [NL-law], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Legal-person business line: relevant manual opt-out call after internal screening. Natural-person professionals/sole traders: prior consent unless the narrow statutory designation branch is actually evidenced.
- **B2C default:** Prior consent; do not rely on the removed general commercial customer-call exception.
- **Default channel basis:** Current11.7(2),(3),(5),(6); natural-person versus legal-person distinction.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Current official text extends consent to other unsolicited communication to natural persons. The customer exception in11.7(4) now derogates from electronic-message paragraph1 only; paragraphs5–6 contain special charity/publisher/lottery branches, not general cold-call permission. The reported 1 July 2026 change-date itself was not independently quoted.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identity and purpose; no caller-ID blocking; easy free objection each natural-person call.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Honour opposition at once; no universal grace verified.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Treating a sole trader as an incorporated company, or relying on an old customer exception, risks violating the current consent text.
- **Evidence:** [NL-law], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Specific prior consent by default; do not stretch published email designation into telephone consent.
- **B2C default:** Prior consent.
- **Default channel basis:** 11.7(1), any11.7(3) exception must be specifically established.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Automated systems without human intervention are covered by11.7(1); corporate status is not a blanket exemption.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on withdrawal/objection.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Consent/classification exposure even where live corporate calls are permitted.
- **Evidence:** [NL-law]. Status: `mixed`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

For identifiable people, document GDPR Art. 6 consent or a purpose/necessity/balancing legitimate-interest assessment where appropriate. Recital 47 is not automatic permission. Art. 14: disclose identity, purposes, basis, source (including whether public), recipients, retention, rights and transfers within one month, or at first contact/disclosure if earlier. Art. 21: stop direct-marketing processing on objection. Minimise fields, verify provenance, agree Art. 28 processor terms, secure data and meet Chapter V transfer rules. Public profiles, enrichment and purchased lists are not exemptions; special-category data needs Art. 9 grounds. Channel consent remains a separate question. [EU-gdpr-original]

Sources: [NL-law], [EU-gdpr-original], [NL-acm-search].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

ACM can impose telecommunications sanctions and orders; the commonly cited €900,000/1% framework was not verified in a primary penalty provision here. Do not use it as a current numeric ceiling until checked. GDPR ceilings are separate.

- **KPN, ACM telemarketing decision reported 2021 — €450,000 reported.** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED: ACM search was access-denied. Reported consumer-call campaign, not one relevant corporate email. Sources: [NL-acm-search].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
