# Sweden: outreach law
Researched 2026-10-22 (JST; 2026-10-21 UTC) by Sol from primary sources where they were reachable. Status: **draft, not yet confirmed by a second reader against the primary texts**. Each rule below is a lead with its source, not a confirmed statement; `../keeping-current.md` says how to confirm one. Every default is a starting value. The owner, or his agent within its grant, can override it; the override line records who and why. An override changes the chosen action, not the law. Scope: ordinary commercial B2B and B2C outreach. Source and version gaps are marked in the text.

**Bottom line:** Legal-person electronic outreach can be opt-out; natural-person/proprietor targeting is consent-based.

Read the EU baseline too: `eu.md`. A GDPR lawful basis and channel permission are separate.

## Channel defaults, legal reasons and override exposure

### Email

- **B2B default:** Opt-out electronic outreach only to a genuine legal-person recipient/address; consent for natural-person recipients, including sole traders and ambiguous named personal accounts.
- **B2C default:** Prior consent; own-customer similar-product exception if19§ conditions fit.
- **Default channel basis:** MarketingAct19§ natural-person consent;20§ applies cessation contact also to legal persons.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis for identifiable people.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Sweden protects natural-person recipients, not just consumers. A named employee address needs classification of the actual addressee, not an automatic assumption that all work email is a corporate addressee. Stored SMS/DM classification should be confirmed; default to consent when unclear.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** 20§ requires a valid cessation address, including legal-person email; stop on objection.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Wrong recipient classification creates a consent issue; enrichment/profiling may independently fail GDPR balancing.
- **Evidence:** [SE-law], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.

### Live human telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Manually call relevant business contacts after internal objections and applicable NIX screening.
- **B2C default:** Consent recommended; otherwise legal opt-out route only after NIX and consumer-sales checks.
- **Default channel basis:** MarketingAct21§ opposition model for other distance communications.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR lawful basis.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** 21§ permits other individual distance communications unless a natural person has clearly opposed the method. NIX rules and consumer written-acceptance requirements are separate and not fully quoted here.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Trader identity, commercial purpose, callback/contact and easy objection.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Stop on objection; no general N-day grace verified.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Unscreened repeat consumer calls can breach marketing rules even if prior consent is not universally mandated.
- **Evidence:** [SE-law], [EU-gdpr-original]. Status: `mixed`.
- **Note:** PRIMARY-QUOTE-UNVERIFIED for NIX operating rules and telephone contract formalities.

### Automated / recorded / artificial-voice telephone calls

- **B2B default:** Consent for natural persons; consent default for legal persons too although19§ is limited to natural persons.
- **B2C default:** Prior consent.
- **Default channel basis:** 19§ automatic systems without a human operator.
- **Data-processing basis:** GDPR consent where personal data involved.
- **Legal rule / uncertainty:** Natural-person automated communication requires prior consent. Do not transfer the21§ live-call model to robocalls.
- **Required content (qualified by scope/verification):** Identify the real sender/principal; do not conceal identity. Provide a valid simple no-cost/ordinary-cost route to stop messages. Supply applicable privacy information at first contact; company/trading disclosures may add postal/registry details.
- **Opt-out legal timing:** Cease on withdrawal/objection.
- **Loop handling:** Suppress further marketing on receipt, across the requested scope; recommended same-day handling. Keep a minimal suppression record, not the full discarded lead profile.
- **If overridden:** Natural-person consent breach; corporate exceptions require specific classification rather than blanket “B2B”.
- **Evidence:** [SE-law]. Status: `mixed`.

## Shared content recommendation — not universal law

Use a truthful sender and company identity. Give a working reply/opt-out route; include a privacy-notice link when personal data is used. For email use a real postal/company address as a loop recommendation even where the channel law does not mandate one. Do not transplant this into a universal SMS/DM address requirement. These are defaults where the local statute does not expressly require the item. A physical postal address is not a universal SMS/DM requirement.

## Lead lists: collection, scraping and enrichment

For identifiable people, document GDPR Art. 6 consent or a purpose/necessity/balancing legitimate-interest assessment where appropriate. Recital 47 is not automatic permission. Art. 14: disclose identity, purposes, basis, source (including whether public), recipients, retention, rights and transfers within one month, or at first contact/disclosure if earlier. Art. 21: stop direct-marketing processing on objection. Minimise fields, verify provenance, agree Art. 28 processor terms, secure data and meet Chapter V transfer rules. Public profiles, enrichment and purchased lists are not exemptions; special-category data needs Art. 9 grounds. Channel consent remains a separate question. [EU-gdpr-original]

Sources: [SE-law], [SE-bonnier-case], [EU-gdpr-original].

## Penalties and actual enforcement record

GDPR ceilings apply to data-law breaches. MarketingAct market-disruption fees, prohibitory orders and conditional fines are separate; exact current monetary scale not quoted here.

- **Bonnier News, IMY26June 2023 — SEK13m.** IMY says upheld by administrative court4February 2025; further appeal pending and leave granted on page last updated3March 2025. Later outcome not checked. Cross-source customer/web-visitor profiling for marketing; not a finding that all cold B2B contact needs consent. Sources: [SE-bonnier-case].

Statutory ceilings, announced settlements, original administrative decisions and money actually collected are different. No probability or expected-loss estimate is asserted. A large campaign/enrichment decision is not a tariff for one ordinary relevant B2B message. No claim of “no enforcement” follows from a missing case.
