# Microsoft 365 and Exchange Online mailboxes

Checked 2026-10-21. This is the outbound side of a Microsoft tenant. The receiving rules for consumer Outlook are in `outlook.md`. Three questions stay apart: law, contract, placement.

## The terms

The Product Terms prohibit use "to spam or distribute malware". Microsoft says bulk sending "isn't a supported use … and is permitted only on a 'best-effort' basis". It gives no Resend-style definition banning every genuine first B2B message.

## Quotas

| limit | value |
|---|---|
| recipients per user per rolling 24 hours | 10,000 |
| messages per minute | 30 |
| recipients per message | configurable up to 1,000 |
| tenant external recipient rate limit (TERRL) | licence-dependent; shown in Exchange admin reports |
| trial tenant | 5,000 external recipients a day |
| default onmicrosoft.com domain | 100 external recipients per organisation per rolling 24 hours |

Recipients are not messages. Vendor tables that say "5,000 Office 365 emails a day" misstate the unit. The planned 2,000 external recipients per mailbox per day limit was cancelled on 6 January 2026; the announcement says it "will not go into effect". TERRL stays.

At the recipient quota the mailbox waits for rolling capacity. SMTP-rate excess is rejected for client retry. Do not retry permanent policy or authentication failures.

## Enforcement

Undisclosed spam and velocity limits can block users or the organisation. Suspected spam leaves through a lower-reputation high-risk delivery pool. Microsoft recommends keeping bulk off the primary domain and using subdomains for marketing and transactional streams: primary-source support for stream separation.

## Loop default

Business correspondence, with low-volume controlled campaigns as an owner-chosen risk at the sending defaults. A secondary domain inside the same tenant does not isolate tenant enforcement.

## If you override

Blocked users or a blocked tenant; mail routed through the high-risk pool. Published quota headroom is not permission.
