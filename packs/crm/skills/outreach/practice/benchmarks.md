# Benchmarks: what each number measures

Checked 2026-10-21. "Delivered" below means accepted by the receiving server unless a study measured inbox placement. 98% accepted is not 98% inbox. Vendor and agency samples are their own clients, not a census of cold email.

## The reports

| report | result | what it measures, and its limits |
|---|---|---|
| Belkins response study, updated 2026-06-26, client campaigns Jan to Dec 2025 | 7,530,489 emails, 34,393 replies; headline 0.45% (raw 0.457%). Companies of 11 to 50 people 0.49%; 10,000 and more 0.22%. Founders and owners 0.57%; C-level 0.42%. | unique replies divided by emails sent, auto-replies and bounces excluded; strictly net-new cold traffic. Earlier Belkins reports divided by openers, so an old 5% and today's 0.45% can describe similar campaigns. Not a positive-reply rate. |
| Instantly benchmark, updated 2026-01-12, data 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-18 | average 3.43%; top quartile 5.5% and above; top decile 10.7% and above. 58% of replies on step one, 42% later. | all replies divided by total emails sent, across thousands of workspaces; no campaign count, no positive or OOO exclusion stated, no list-type split. A different sample and numerator from Belkins. |
| Belkins deliverability study, updated 2026-06-29, same base | 128,605 bounces of 7,530,489 sends: 1.71%; unsubscribes 0.36%. By country: India 3.26%, Spain 2.86%, Sweden 1.05%, Finland 0.71%. | hard and soft bounces together, over sends. Not complaints and not placement. Client mix confounds the country figures. |
| Woodpecker article, updated 2026-02-24 | replies average 3.43%, good 5 to 10%, excellent 10% and up; bounces average 5.1%, good under 2%; deeper personalisation 17 to 18% against 7 to 9% | a mix of its own older observations and other studies, not one cohort. Its 3.43% is not independent confirmation of Instantly's. |
| Gong Labs 2024 recap, published 2025-01-10, modified 2026-05-27 | calling associated with email replies of 3.44% against 1.81%; voicemail 5.87% against 2.73% | 300 million calls, observed, not randomised. Supports testing phone beside email; proves no multiplier. |
| one forum post, 2026-03-05, local-business campaign | 154 contacts, 16 replies (about 10.4%), 11 positive (about 7.1%) | a small self-selected report; contacts, not sends, in the denominator; not a SaaS or enterprise forecast |

## For budgeting

Use a broad 0.5% to 3.5% total replies per email sent, the span of the two contemporary reports. Not a pooled average, not a confidence interval, not a forecast. A tight small cohort can beat it; some valid enterprise motions fall below it. Under Instantly's definitions 5% and up is strong and 10% and up is top tier. A campaign can look good on total replies and be commercially poor if the replies are negative.

Track human positive replies per unique delivered prospect, and qualified held meetings per unique delivered prospect, beside the total. No universal positive-reply or meeting rate by list type exists in the sources. Set the target from deal value, qualification and the campaign's own history, not an invented "2% positive".

## By list type

| list | what the sources support | pilot expectation |
|---|---|---|
| hand-researched, current, tightly relevant | Belkins' net-new 0.45%; small practitioner cohorts far higher; neither isolates the effect of hand research | under 1% bounces; 5% human replies per delivered prospect as an aspiration, not a rule |
| recently verified database contacts | Apollo's 91% accuracy claim under its own definition; no like-for-like complaint comparison | the same bounce lines; "verified" waives nothing |
| catch-all, risky, unknown | no trustworthy matched benchmark | a separate small pilot; no expected rate |
| old, scraped, bought, unverified | different quality dimensions, not one measured class; several sending services prohibit them | requalify and reverify first |
| opted-in or existing customers | not cold outreach; its rates do not transfer | report separately |

No measured complaint baseline by list type exists. Use the provider's own rate, aim well under 0.1%, and investigate a single event on a small cohort. An unsubscribe rate is not a complaint rate.
