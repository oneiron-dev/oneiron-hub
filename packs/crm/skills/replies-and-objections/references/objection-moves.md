# Objection moves

The move is what the line does. Write the words for this person. Examples use varied industries on purpose.

## The listening spine

1. **Echo.** Repeat the two or three words that carried the weight, as a question. Then wait. The silence does the asking; it replaces "what do you mean?"
2. **Name it.** "Seems like the timing is the real problem." Offer a reading they can correct; never tell them what they feel. Naming a worry shrinks it; naming a hope grows it.
3. **Empty the stack.** Ask what else is on the list before you answer any of it.
4. **Play it back.** Summarise their facts and their feelings in their own framing until they confirm you have it exactly. A polite "fair enough" is not that; it is them closing the door.

## Questions that move

Open with "what" and "how." Avoid questions a yes or no can end.

- "What about this matters most to you?"
- "What happens if you do nothing?" / "What does doing nothing cost you?"
- "How does this land with the people not on this call?"
- "What's stopping you from starting now?"
- "How will we know we're on track?"
- Hand an impossible demand back as a question: "Help me see how that works on my side."
- Ask so that "no" is safe to say: "Is this a bad week for it?" People relax after a no, and the real talk starts.

## "We have a vendor" / "the spreadsheet works"

Validate strongly first. "Sounds like it's doing the job. That's rare." Wait. They volunteer the gap. Then one neutral question about the failure mode. Then a before-and-after from a similar customer. Then a tiny ask. Never "a lot of people felt that way, but..."

## "Too expensive"

- Echo: "...too expensive?" Wait. Name it: "seems like price is the main concern." Then: "What else?"
- Then: "Sounds like you're comparing it to something. What?"
- Never reflex-match. You don't know their scope or whether the cheap bid is a loss leader. Matching reprices your whole book to the worst bid.
- Reframe the comparison set. Name what the cheap option actually is. Usually a different category: rented labour versus an owned outcome. The question becomes "which do you need?"
- Open a knowledge gap with one diagnostic question the cheap bid cannot answer. "How will they handle {{the specific risk you surfaced on the call}}?"
- Name the risk gap in their units. "A wrong {{their core number}} costs you {{their figure}}. The difference in price is insurance on that."
- Say so when cheap is right. "If you only need {{the narrow thing}}, take the cheaper one." Authority against your own interest buys credibility.
- Structural prevention: a named process at a flat price has no line items to compare; a guarantee carries the risk the cheap bid leaves with them. Decline requests for proposals by default; they exist to commoditise you.
- State the price with confidence. Defend it by what it buys, never by apologising.

Example, a landscaping firm against a half-price bid:

> "Set price aside for a second. Which of us do you trust to get it right?" ... "What makes you say that?" ... "When did a cheaper choice last end up costing you more?"

## "I need to think about it"

The scale: "One to ten, ten is you're ready today, one is you wouldn't take it free. Where are you?" Then: "What would make it a ten?"

## "I need to check with my partner / boss"

Find the likely no before they carry it over: "If it comes back as a no, which part will it be about: the scope, the fee or the timing?" Then offer to join or pre-brief that conversation.

## "No budget" (early)

"No problem. I mostly want to understand how you handle X. If we're relevant, there's time for budget later."

## "Send me some information"

"Happy to. I'll do it properly and it'll take me half an hour to make it about you. Can I take thirty seconds to check it's even relevant first?"

## "Can we do a trial?"

A guided session with success criteria, never an open sandbox. A trial with no usage design proves nothing.

## Trust and bad reviews

Acknowledge, give the specific context, pivot to the volume of proof. Trust objections are answered with assets: references, an audit, a guided session. Not with words.

## Pre-empt the usual reasons

Before booking the next call: "Mind if I share why people usually don't go ahead? Timing, priority, budget, a preference for someone else. Any of those live here?"

## When you catch yourself pitching

Name it and hand the floor back. "I've started talking about us. Back to you: what happened next?"

## The clean exit

Say it is handled on their side, thank them, and go. "Looks like this is sorted at your end. Thanks for the time; I won't chase." Leave the door open; a polite decline earns the referral later.
