# Question library

Written to be asked out loud. Swap in their words.

## Replacements for the two forbidden questions

Instead of "would you buy this?":

- "How do you handle this today?"
- "What does that cost you in time or money each month?"
- "When did it last come up? Walk me through it."
- "Have you looked for something to fix it? What did you find?"
- If not: "What stopped you looking?"

Instead of "how much would you pay?":

- "What does the problem cost you?"
- "What do you pay today to deal with it, in tools or people?"
- "Where would the budget for a fix come from?"

After "I'd definitely buy that":

- "When did this last bite you?"
- "What did you try?"
- "Did you search for anything? What came up?"

## Story prompts (open discovery)

- "Tell me about the last time you {{did the activity}}."
- "Start at the beginning. What happened first?"
- "Where were you? Who was with you?"
- "What happened next?"
- "What got in the way?"
- "What did you do with the result the next morning?"

## Discovery sequence

1. The last time it happened.
2. What changed since.
3. How often it happens.
4. The manual steps.
5. The tools.
6. Why you switched, if you did.
7. The part you like least.
8. If you could change one thing.
9. Midpoint: "Is there anything else I should know?" Then silence.
10. "Who else should I talk to?"
11. "What should I have asked?"

## Switch sequence (business)

1. How the need came up.
2. What you hoped for.
3. What was unclear.
4. What else you looked at.
5. Who weighed in.
6. What nearly stopped you.

## Cancellation sequence

1. What led up to it.
2. Why you signed up in the first place.
3. What you expected to see and didn't.
4. What you use instead now.
5. Nothing about coming back.

## Usability

"We're testing the product, not you. Say everything you're thinking as you go, even the dull bits."

## Feature requests

- "If you had that, what would it let you do?"
- "How do you cope without it?"
- "Why does that matter now?"

## Probes

- "Why do you bother?"
- "What happens if you do nothing?"
- "What else have you tried?"
- "Where does the money come from?"
- "What's the most annoying part of your morning?"

## Observation triggers

Any one of these marks an opportunity:

- a task done more than once a day or week
- copy-paste between tools
- visible exasperation at a question
- constant tab switching
- pulling out a phone mid-task

## The rejection loop (pre-sales)

- "Why not?"
- "Who would want this?"
- "What would make it a no-brainer?"
- "What would you pay for that version?"

## The cold meeting ask (five beats, under the interview-ask cap)

```
Hi {{first_name}},

I'm trying to make {{the painful thing}} less of a chore. We're early and have nothing to sell. The part I don't understand is {{the specific gap}}. You've done {{their thing}} for years and could cut through it fast.

Could I borrow {{call_length}} next week? I'm free {{two windows}}. This is research, not a sales call.

{{sender_first_name}}
```
